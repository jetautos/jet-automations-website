Aircraft & Instructors

Training aircraft are available at Pleasanton Municipal Airport, and certified flight instructors are on staff. You train where you fly, with no commuting to a big-city flight line.

A Part 61 flight school

Eagle Aviation trains under FAA Part 61, which means the program is built around you instead of a fixed classroom schedule. Your instructor shapes the plan to your goals, your pace, and your life. The certificate you earn is exactly the same.

  • Your schedule. Train evenings, weekends, or full-time. The pace is yours.
  • One-on-one. Every lesson is flown with your instructor and tailored to how you learn.
  • No wasted hours. The syllabus flexes to your progress instead of a rigid timeline.
  • Start to finish. One plan from your first lesson to your checkride, held to the same FAA standards as any school.

Every student is different. Contact us to talk through your goals, your schedule, and the right path for you.

Pleasanton Municipal Airport · KPEZ

A growing home field

Eagle Aviation trains at Pleasanton Municipal Airport, a city-owned field south of San Antonio, and one in the middle of a major expansion.

499 Acres Acquired

The City of Pleasanton has purchased roughly 499 acres adjacent to the field to make room for a major airport expansion.

An Expanded Runway

The centerpiece of the plan is a realigned, expanded runway, developed with TxDOT Aviation using state and federal funds, a project the city estimates at around $44 million.

New Hangars & Fuel Farm

Federal investment is already on the ground, with recent groundbreakings for a new fuel farm and a hangar with space for up to eight aircraft, backed by $1.71 million in USDA funding.

A growing field means more aircraft, more hangar space, and more room to train. Get started.

About Eagle Aviation

Eagle Aviation is a veteran-owned flight school, founded and led by Clint Powell — a U.S. Air Force Reserve veteran and career pilot with decades of experience across flight instruction, charter, corporate, and airline flying. He previously served as mayor of Pleasanton, his longtime home.

Clint served twelve years in the U.S. Air Force Reserve, retiring as a staff sergeant, and continues to serve in the Texas State Guard. He understands the discipline, structure, and standards that military aviators bring to the cockpit — and what it takes to build on that foundation in civilian training.

Clint has taught as a certificated flight instructor (CFI), instrument instructor (CFII), and multi-engine instructor (MEI). He has flown as a Part 135 and corporate captain, and as an airline pilot with Mesa Airlines and Frontier Airlines. He also holds an FAA Part 107 remote pilot certificate.

That experience sets the standard for how Eagle Aviation trains. Clint owns and leads the school, and the certified flight instructors he has brought on handle day-to-day instruction — carrying the habits, judgment, and standards of professional flying into every lesson.

The VMAX Program

VMAX, a national initiative training high school students in aviation through a four-year pathway that produces certificated flight instructors. Students train in T-6 simulators modeled on U.S. Air Force training standards, and the program launches as a collegiate competition in 2028.
